Planta: what it does, and what Plant Care takes from it

Source: Planta's App Store listing (id1410126781, 4.8 from 115K ratings, Editors' Choice, subtitle "Identifier, Diagnose & Journal"). These are marketing screenshots, so they show the strongest screens, not every step. The onboarding itself wasn't visible and isn't described from guesswork here.

Why Planta looks designed and our "Care Card" can look AI-made

  • Planta commits to one colour. Pale sage background, deep forest-green ink for every title, solid green buttons. You'd know it with the logo covered. Care Card's beige paper, muted brown-grey ink and cream cards is the default "tasteful" palette of AI-generated interfaces right now; it's identity by absence.
  • Planta's type has weight. A heavy grotesk for titles and plant names, regular for the rest. Care Card's light serif titles plus small grey text look polite and generic.
  • Planta uses solid colour blocks with meaning. Watering is a navy card, fertilising a mustard one. Care Card puts everything in identical white rows.
  • Photos are big and carry labels. Room, condition and a warning sit as pills on the photo. Care Card keeps photos small in list rows.
  • Each screen has one big answer. "Part sun", "Regular watering in 12 days", "Identification completed".

Where Plant Care is different (and must stay different)

  • Planta tells you to water. Plant Care waters. Planta's core is a reminder schedule with a checkbox ("Regular watering in 12 days · Last: 2 days ago"). Ours is a measured state: "Watered itself 2 days ago · water left for about 9 days". No checkbox, no chore.
  • Don't take its colour. Planta owns sage and forest green; PictureThis and PlantIn own dark UI with mint. A third green app disappears. Below are two non-green brand colours: Clay (the pot) and Water (what the module does).
  • Leave out: community feed, experts, Meta glasses, reviews and laurels in the product, the "Dr" hub. They're Planta's growth engine, not our job.
  • Rooms: Planta groups by room. Our Home groups by "needs you" first, because a module problem matters more than where the plant stands. Room stays as a label on the photo.

D, one colour: ink and photos

Owner feedback: the colours fight. Cause: up to four solid colours on one screen (brand, clay alert, navy water card, dark-grey offline pill). Fix: the interface is ink on off-white, with no brand hue. One colour, clay red, is used only when something needs you. Water shows as a thin blue level, not a solid card. The photos are the colour.

Colour rules: ink for all text, buttons and the selected tab; clay red only for "needs you" (pill, outline on the card at fault); water blue only inside the level bar. Offline is an outlined pill, because waiting for a module isn't an alarm.

D, neutral page: photos first

Owner feedback: the photos fight with the tinted page. Fix: the page is a neutral off-white (#F5F5F3, no hue), so any photo sits cleanly whatever its walls, light or pot. Brand colour lives only in titles, buttons, the selected tab and the water card. On the plant page the photo runs edge to edge under the status bar, so it's never framed by page colour.

D1 Clay

  • Brand ink: burnt clay #5A2416 on a pale clay tint #F6EAE3; white cards.
  • Warm, earthy, tied to the physical pot. No plant app uses terracotta as its brand.
  • Risk: alerts are clay-red too, so they must be the only solid bright clay; brand stays dark.
